Feature lists on cheat sites tend to read like buzzword soup. This is what each part of the menu actually does, and when you'd use it. The menu opens with INSERT and has three tabs: Visuals, Aimbot and Misc.

## Aimbot

The aim doesn't snap. It eases toward an operator inside a circle you set around your crosshair, and it ignores anyone behind a wall. You choose head, upper chest or closest. You can cap the distance, skip teammates, skip downed operators, and draw the circle on screen so you can see what it covers.

Best for: players who lose gunfights on tracking, not on positioning. Details in the [aimbot settings guide](/blog/mw4-aimbot-setup/).

## Triggerbot

Fires when your crosshair is already on an operator. It has a short delay, a head or upper-chest choice, and the same wall check as the aim. It never moves your crosshair, so some players use it alone. See [triggerbot and recoil](/blog/mw4-triggerbot/).

## Player visuals

Box or corner box, skeleton, health, armor plates, distance and the weapon in someone's hands. Names are off until you turn them on. Someone behind cover gets a quieter color than someone you can see. Your squad can have its own color, and a distance cap keeps 6v6 readable. Setup tips are in the [ESP and radar guide](/blog/mw4-esp-setup/).

## Radar

A 2D radar with operator markers, adjustable range and zoom. Same information as the player visuals, just on a small map.

## Loot (Hajin DMZ)

Cash, bags, armor, ammo, contracts, extract points and vehicles along the farmland roads. This layer stays off in 6v6. See the [DMZ guide](/blog/mw4-dmz-guide/).

## Misc

No recoil (a light settle on rifle recoil), no spread, a stream-proof overlay, one saved config for 6v6 and another for DMZ, and a custom crosshair.

## What to turn on first

If you're new, start with player visuals and a distance cap. Add radar if the screen feels busy. Add aim assist last, with a small circle.
