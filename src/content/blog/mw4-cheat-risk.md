Every seller says their product is safe. You deserve the real picture before you pay.

## What we can't promise

We can't promise you won't be banned. Publishers update their detection regularly and review player reports. Anyone selling "100% undetectable" is selling a slogan.

## What changes your risk

**How you play.** This matters most. Instant lock-ons, perfect accuracy, shooting through walls the second someone appears and tracking people with no audio cue all get reported. Other players ban cheaters faster than software does.

**Which features you use.** Passive information, such as radar and player visuals, leaves less obvious traces in gameplay than heavy aim assistance.

**Your settings.** A small aim circle, slow smoothing and a delay on the triggerbot look much closer to normal play. See the [aimbot settings guide](/blog/mw4-aimbot-setup/).

**Timing.** Right after a patch is riskier. Use the [patch day checklist](/blog/mw4-patch-day/).

**Where the software comes from.** Official downloads only.

## Free downloads are a worse bet

- **Malware.** Free cheat downloads are a common way people end up with stealers on their PC. A cheat asks for admin rights and antivirus exclusions, which is also what malware wants.
- **Widely known.** A free, widely shared tool is easy for anti-cheat to collect and flag.
- **No updates, no support.** Games patch. Free tools usually don't follow.

Paid doesn't make it risk-free. It does mean updates, a status page and someone to ask when it breaks. Plans start at $35 a month.

## Sensible precautions

- Don't use an account you can't afford to lose.
- Start with fewer features and smaller settings.
- Don't launch when the [status page](/status/) says an update is in progress.
- Don't talk about it in lobbies.

## The bottom line

Using cheats carries risk and you're the one who accepts it. If you're comfortable with that, we'll give you a good tool, clear guidance and honest updates. If you're not, don't buy.
