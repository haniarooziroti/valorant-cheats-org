The number one mistake with ESP is turning everything on. You end up staring at boxes, bars and lines instead of the actual game. 6v6 maps are tight, so clutter hurts even more than in bigger modes.

## Start with three things

1. Box or corner box
2. Distance
3. Health

That's enough to know who's where, how far, and whether they're worth pushing. Corner boxes take up less screen than full boxes, so try those first.

## Add things one at a time

After a few matches, add armor plates if you want to know how much damage someone can take. Add the weapon in hand if you're deciding whether to hold an angle against a shotgun. Skeleton is useful when you want to read stance, but it adds a lot of lines, so keep it for when you want it.

Names stay off unless you specifically need them.

## Colors

The tool already uses a quieter color for someone behind cover and a stronger one for someone you can see. Pick colors that stand apart from the map. Your squad can have its own color too, which stops you from misreading a teammate.

## Distance cap

Set it. In 6v6, the information past a reasonable range is mostly noise. The menu goes up to 300m, but you rarely need anything close to that for the launch maps.

## Radar

The radar is a 2D map with operator markers, adjustable range and zoom. Put it where you already glance at the in-game mini-map. Use a shorter range in 6v6 and a longer one in Ground War, where fights are spread out.

## ESP or radar?

| | ESP | Radar |
|---|---|---|
| Screen clutter | Medium to high | Low |
| Precision | High | Moderate |
| Good in tight maps | Yes | OK |
| Good in big maps | Yes | Yes |

Plenty of players run radar plus a light ESP and nothing else.

## Test before you queue

Load a private match and make sure the overlay lines up, then check your frame rate. If it drops, remove skeleton and snaplines first.
